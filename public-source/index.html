Households, consumption and consumer prices

Look closer at what people can afford.

Compare the resources households receive, the consumption recorded for them and the prices they face. Every measure keeps its own population, denominator, source period and price basis.

Three lenses on material living standards

Income: disposable-income accounts and household income surveys describe different income concepts. A median describes the middle of a distribution; a mean describes its average. Equivalised income adjusts for household size, while per-person survey values use a different denominator.

Consumption: household surveys and national accounts describe different spending or consumption concepts. The national-account measure includes nonprofit institutions serving households. Per-person values are averages, and consumption alone does not establish what every household can afford.

Consumer prices: the consumption price-level index compares countries with the United States = 100 in each year. A within-country consumer price index tracks price changes over time. Its base-year index levels cannot rank countries by living costs, and a relative international price level can change with exchange rates.

Survey periods and known comparability breaks stay visible. The interface does not interpolate missing years, blend income and consumption survey definitions, or construct a universal household budget. Published estimates and analyst-calculated adjustments are labeled beside their source values.

Housing, food, home energy and healthcare

See the accounts behind essential costs.

Compare selected consumption categories with disposable household income, inspect the original inputs, and follow the same definitions through time. Estimates and published source values stay distinct.

Estimated resources after selected essentials

This national-account comparison uses compatible household-sector income and resident consumption where available. Category shares are observed on a domestic basis and proportionally allocated to resident consumption. The result estimates resources for other spending and saving; it is not a household cash balance, measured saving rate or complete needs budget.

Housing includes imputed housing services and excludes home energy in the separate housing component, so home energy is counted once. Gross income is before consumption of fixed capital; net income is after it. Direct taxes are already accounted for in disposable income. COICOP means Classification of Individual Consumption According to Purpose. Its 1999 and 2018 editions remain separate, and an exact country/year stays with every comparison.

How to read evidence

A useful number needs its context.

The aim is to make questions inspectable, including the assumptions and missing evidence that could change an explanation.

01

Check what is being measured.

Read the unit, geography, reporting population and period. A stock is different from annual production. A named pension plan’s investment return is different from a household’s pension payment. A state total does not describe every resident.

02

Keep unavailable values unavailable.

Suppressed, not reported, below publication precision and zero are different. Status labels stay with the records. Published estimates and imputations are identified where the source provides them.

03

Look through time and across places.

Changes in reporting coverage, prices, denominators or borders can change a comparison. A graph joins adjacent measured years only; no missing years or numerical cells are invented.

04

Separate a pattern from an explanation.

A relationship can motivate a hypothesis. It does not by itself identify what caused it. Ask about competing explanations, comparison groups, confounding, timing and uncertainty.

05

Keep models explicit.

A conditional calculation shows what follows under its assumptions. It is different from a forecast or a measured policy effect. Model choices, input data, validation and sensitivity need to remain visible.

06

Make room to remain uncertain.

You can change your mind, keep your view, or say you need more evidence. A public preference is not an answer key for an empirical question, and an empirical pattern does not settle every value judgment.

What this public library contains

The full catalogue indexes the retained measures from the wider research workbench. The complete retained numerical source records are available through the public explorer and downloads. Original-source links, units, year ranges and source definitions remain attached.

Coverage varies by collection and measure; it is stated beside the values. A retained source panel can cover selected places or reporting years and does not by itself form a complete worldwide shipment ledger. Monetary input–output connections, physical goods movements and emissions accounting allocations keep separate meanings.

The desktop research environment retains additional analytical tools alongside the public numerical records and supporting source downloads. Availability in the catalogue does not mean a regression, causal analysis or new economic model has been run.

Real-world data and comparisons between countries

Map values come from retained publisher records, with original source identifiers, years, units and status flags. The world maps draw on the complete retained country-measure catalogue and additional historical collections. Each selected measure shows its actual source years, coverage and retained observations; the full explorer and earlier evidence files remain available.

Reported, estimated and source-imputed values stay distinct. The FAOSTAT records include official figures, estimates, values imputed by the receiving agency, external-organization figures and missing cells. Their native labels remain in country summaries and downloads. The interface does not fill missing values or turn them into zero. A hypothetical arithmetic example explains a definition; it never supplies a map or model input.

A common number format is not enough for a valid comparison. Match the commodity or industry definition, source year, unit, geography, population or denominator, and source edition. Physical tonnes, per-person values and current dollars answer different questions. Purchasing-power or inflation adjustments require a documented source and transformation. Latest-available country snapshots can contain different years and are not annual histories.

Annual histories and single-year snapshots remain separate. Source collections cover different periods and countries; use the selected measure’s source-year selector and coverage notes to check what is retained. Latest-available country snapshots can mix reporting years and do not become an annual playback sequence. Missing years and values remain unavailable.

The existing economic model also uses sourced accounts. Its OECD ICIO 2025 inputs cover 2020–2022, 80 named economies plus a rest-of-world account, and 50 industries, in million current U.S. dollars at basic prices. A configured demand change is a conditional model scenario, separate from those source inputs. The household supplement adds source-defined income, consumption and consumer-price context. The Prices & PPP supplement adds explicit inflation-reporting indices and published national purchasing-power comparisons; the original industry model remains in its source units. The environmental demand-response extension remains unavailable because its reconstruction did not meet the source-rounding check; descriptive emissions accounts remain separate.

Private notes and research participation

Reading and exploring do not submit a response. Optional notes here are stored only in this browser tab and can be downloaded by you. They are not uploaded, counted in a poll, or sent to Civic Voice.

Civic Voice is a separate, access-controlled companion with its own explanation of saving and consent. Notes made in this library are not automatically compatible with its current chapter-review storage.

Voluntary participant responses can describe what participants report. They do not automatically represent a state, a country or everyone who visits. A before/after self-report alone does not establish that the interface caused learning.
