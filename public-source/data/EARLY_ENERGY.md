# Earlier energy histories

Prepared 1 November 2026. Existing histories are preserved; source branches are separate and are never silently stitched together.

Country maps use exact source country identities or documented name aliases. Original historical countries, regions, world accounts and uncertain source labels remain in the source-defined collection rather than being allocated to successors. Modern polygons are a navigation aid, not evidence of unchanged historical borders.

The Etemad & Luciani branch contains historical production estimates (Mtoe) from 1900–1979, published in World Energy Production 1800–1985 (1991) and distributed by The Shift Project with author agreement. This branch and its normalization are **CC BY-SA 3.0**. Attribution: Etemad & Luciani (1991), via The Shift Project, accessed 1 November 2026. Every repeated native country/fuel/year key remains unavailable, with all original rows attached; no sum, average or arbitrary choice resolves conflicting values.

EIA international annual histories use separately declared native physical/energy units and actual country reporting years. Selected original annual series lines contain all native observations, unavailable source symbols and series metadata. The full current publisher bulk download is linked in the register, with its acquired checksum. Monthly, quarterly and alternative-unit source series are outside this selected evidence branch, not deleted from any existing collection.

U.S. MER production and consumption histories use only the publisher annual month-13 rows; monthly data are retained in the original downloads and are not summed locally. U.S. crude-oil annual volume and annual-average daily production remain separate parallel measures. The sparse 1635–1945 benchmark collection uses source-defined historical U.S. geography and publisher decade-average fuelwood assignments; it is not an annual country-map series. The source explicitly documents a fuelwood-only/wood-and-derived-fuels discontinuity between 1945 and 1949.

No missing value, withheld cell, source symbol or duplicate ambiguity is converted to zero. No local numerical unit conversion, historical border allocation, interpolation or join across source editions is performed.
